# Step 2 — Design Thesis

## Objective

Create a distinctive conceptual direction before selecting detailed visual treatments.

## Inputs

- Discovery Snapshot

## Actions

1. Define the intended emotional response.
2. Identify 2–4 visual/conceptual themes.
3. Choose one dominant design concept.
4. Define the composition philosophy.
5. Define how the site should feel when static.
6. Define the role of imagery.
7. Define the role of motion at a conceptual level.
8. Identify the obvious/template solution.
9. Decide whether and why to reject it.

## Design Thesis Format

Produce:

**Design thesis**

One concise paragraph describing the concept.

**Visual principles**

3–6 principles.

**Composition principle**

How space, scale, alignment, imagery, and hierarchy work together.

**Motion principle**

How movement should feel and what it communicates.

**Distinctiveness**

What makes the design recognizable without relying on the logo.

**Anti-patterns**

Patterns explicitly rejected for this project.

## Critical Questions

- Would another AI likely produce this same concept?
- Is the design concept strong without animation?
- Is novelty coming from a genuine idea or visual gimmicks?
- Does the concept fit the audience and objective?
- Could the visual direction become exhausting after several sections?

## Output

`Design Thesis`

## Stop Conditions

Do not proceed if the concept is merely a list of adjectives such as "modern, clean, premium."

A concept must explain **how** the design communicates.

## Self-Review

Write:

> "The weakest part of this concept is..."

Then improve it if the weakness is material.
